\paragraph{Is the mismatch merely a scalar confidence error?}
Teacher--student output discrepancies may reflect differences in overall confidence rather than class-relative structure.
We test this explanation using an oracle that independently rescales each student's logit vector to minimize its KL divergence to the fixed teacher distribution.
On all 10,000 CIFAR-100 test images, using the historical ResNet32$\times$4--ResNet8$\times$4 linear-KD checkpoint (seed 2051), calibration reduces the mean weighted divergence from 1.2198 to 0.5575 ($8\,\mathrm{KL}$ at $T=4$; Fig.~\ref{fig:oracle_calib_cifar}).
Thus, 45.7\% of the original mean discrepancy remains even under this per-image oracle, ruling out a purely positive-scalar explanation for this endpoint.
However, a same-state control shows that replacing the affine readout with angular scores, while fixing the features, classifier directions, and teacher targets, does not reduce the mean calibrated residual (0.5575 versus 0.5601; Fig.~\ref{fig:oracle_same_state_cifar}).
These observations establish a mismatch beyond scalar confidence, but do not uniquely identify classifier geometry as its cause or show that post-hoc normalization resolves it; we therefore examine the effect of training with an angular interface separately.
