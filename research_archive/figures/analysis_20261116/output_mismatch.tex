\paragraph{Output mismatch beyond scalar confidence.}
We first investigate whether the discrepancy between teacher and student outputs can be explained solely by their overall confidence scales.
Using a frozen linear-KD student, we perform an oracle-calibration probe: for each image, we independently optimize a nonnegative scalar multiplying the entire student logit vector to minimize its KL divergence to the fixed teacher distribution.
This per-image oracle is more flexible than a single global temperature and therefore provides a stringent test of the scalar-confidence explanation.
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:output_mismatch}(a), on all 10,000 CIFAR-100 test images, calibration reduces the mean weighted divergence from 1.2198 to 0.5575 ($8\,\mathrm{KL}$ at $T=4$), leaving 45.7\% of the original mean discrepancy.
The post-calibration median is 0.4260, indicating that the residual is not confined to a few extreme examples.
Consistently, the centered teacher and calibrated student class scores still deviate from exact agreement in Fig.~\ref{fig:output_mismatch}(b).
These observations demonstrate that, for this endpoint, matching overall confidence is insufficient to recover the teacher's class-relative outputs, motivating a closer examination of the classifier parameterization.
